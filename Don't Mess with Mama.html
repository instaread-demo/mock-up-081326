If you’ve seen The Pink Stuff in the store, you know why cleaning pastes have become so popular. That thick, scrubby texture is great for messes that need a little more elbow grease.

But you can make your own DIY cleaning paste at home with baking soda, dish soap, hydrogen peroxide, and optional essential oil.

It takes less than five minutes to mix together and works especially well on tough kitchen and bathroom messes.

What Is a Cleaning Paste?

A cleaning paste is a thick, mildly abrasive cleaner that you apply directly to a surface and scrub.

Unlike a spray cleaner, the thick consistency stays where you put it and gives you a little more scrubbing power for stuck-on food, grease, soap scum, stains, and grime.

I especially like it for stubborn messes that need a little extra scrubbing power.

Why You’ll Love This DIY Cleaning Paste

  • Takes less than 5 minutes to make
  • Uses simple ingredients
  • Thick and scoopable
  • Helps tackle grease, grime, and stuck-on messes
  • Great for kitchens and bathrooms
  • Easy to make in small batches

About the Ingredients

You only need a few basic cleaning ingredients to make this homemade cleaning paste.

Baking Soda

Baking soda gives this cleaning paste its thick consistency and scrubbing power. It’s a mild abrasive that helps loosen stuck-on food, soap scum, grease, and grime.

Dish Soap

Dish soap helps cut through grease and gives the baking soda a smoother, spreadable consistency.

I prefer dish soap over liquid castile soap for this recipe because it’s made to cut through grease and rinses away easily.

Hydrogen Peroxide

I add a small amount of 3% hydrogen peroxide to help with stains and brighten dingy surfaces. It also helps create the right consistency for the paste.

Start with 1 tablespoon and add another tablespoon if needed. Different dish soaps have different consistencies, so you may need a little more or less liquid to get the texture you want.

Lemon Essential Oil (Optional)

I like adding Lemon essential oil for a fresh, clean scent. You can leave it out or use another essential oil you like for cleaning.

DIY Cleaning Paste Recipe

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup baking soda
  • 1/4 cup liquid dish soap
  • 1–2 tablespoons 3% hydrogen peroxide
  • 5–10 drops Lemon essential oil (optional)

Instructions

  1. Add the baking soda and dish soap to a small mixing bowl.
  2. Add 1 tablespoon of hydrogen peroxide and stir until combined.
  3. If the mixture is too thick, add up to 1 additional tablespoon of hydrogen peroxide until you get a thick, scoopable paste.
  4. Add the Lemon essential oil, if using, and stir again.
  5. Transfer the cleaning paste to a small jar with a lid.

The finished mixture should be thick enough to scoop from the jar but easy to spread onto a surface.

Yield: About 1 cup
Uses: About 10–15 uses (1 tablespoon per use)

Estimated Cost: About $1 per batch

How to Use DIY Cleaning Paste

Scoop a small amount onto a damp sponge, cleaning cloth, or soft scrub brush.

Apply it to the surface and gently scrub in circular motions. For stubborn grime, let the paste sit for a few minutes before scrubbing.

Rinse the surface well with water or wipe several times with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining residue.

You don’t need much. Start with a small amount and add more if needed.

Where to Use Cleaning Paste

I like using this cleaning paste on stainless steel sinks, porcelain sinks, tubs, showers, tile, grout, glass cooktops, oven doors, cookware, and other hard surfaces that need a little extra scrubbing.

Because surfaces and finishes can vary, always test the cleaning paste in a small, inconspicuous spot first.

Surfaces to Avoid

I don’t recommend using this cleaning paste on natural stone such as marble, granite, limestone, or travertine. I would also avoid unsealed wood, painted or waxed surfaces, and delicate or high-gloss finishes that may scratch.

Hydrogen peroxide can have a mild bleaching effect, so be careful around colored fabrics and other surfaces that could discolor.

When in doubt, test a small hidden area first.

DIY Cleaning Paste vs. Soft Scrub

My DIY cleaning paste and Homemade Soft Scrub use similar ingredients, but the consistency is different.

Soft Scrub contains more dish soap, so it’s creamy and loose enough to keep in a squeeze bottle. I like it for everyday cleaning around the kitchen and bathroom, especially in the toilet bowl.

This cleaning paste uses more baking soda in relation to the dish soap, so it’s much thicker and scoopable. I keep it in a jar and use it when I want more scrubbing power for a stubborn mess.

The cleaning paste also contains hydrogen peroxide, which can help with stains and brighten dingy surfaces.

Soft Scrub = creamy and squeezable

Cleaning Paste = thick and scoopable

How to Store Cleaning Paste

Because this recipe contains hydrogen peroxide, I recommend making a small batch rather than making a large amount to store for months.

Keep it tightly covered in a cool, dark place. Hydrogen peroxide gradually breaks down when exposed to light and after it’s mixed with other ingredients, so the fresher the paste is, the better.

If the paste separates while sitting, give it a quick stir before using.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this the same as The Pink Stuff?

No. This isn’t a copycat of the exact Pink Stuff formula. It’s a simple homemade cleaning paste that I use for many of the same types of tough cleaning jobs.

Why do you add hydrogen peroxide?

Hydrogen peroxide can help lift certain stains and brighten dingy surfaces. It also helps give the paste a smooth, workable consistency.

Should I use 1 or 2 tablespoons of hydrogen peroxide?

Start with 1 tablespoon. If the paste is still too thick, add the second tablespoon a little at a time until you like the consistency.

The exact amount can vary depending on how thick your dish soap is.

Can I leave out the hydrogen peroxide?

Yes. You can make a basic cleaning paste with baking soda and dish soap. You may need to adjust the amount of dish soap slightly to get the right consistency.

Can I use this on stainless steel?

I use it on stainless steel sinks, but always test it first. Use a soft sponge or cloth, scrub gently with the grain, and rinse well.

Can I use this on a glass cooktop?

I use it for stuck-on messes on glass cooktops, but check your manufacturer’s cleaning instructions first and test it in a small area. Avoid aggressive scrubbing that could scratch the surface.

Can I use this on natural stone?

No. I don’t recommend this recipe for marble, granite, limestone, travertine, or other natural stone.

How long does DIY cleaning paste last?

Because the recipe contains hydrogen peroxide, I prefer to make it in small batches and use it relatively quickly rather than storing it for months.

Final Thoughts

This DIY cleaning paste is one of those simple recipes that’s worth keeping around for tougher cleaning jobs.

The combination of baking soda, dish soap, and hydrogen peroxide creates a thick, scoopable scrub that’s great for tackling grease, soap scum, stains, and stuck-on grime.

It takes just a few minutes to make, and you can easily mix up a small batch whenever you need it.
