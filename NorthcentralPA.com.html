Contractor accused of exploiting undocumented workers

Montoursville, Pa. — The owner of a Montoursville home improvement company is accused of running a labor trafficking scheme that exploited undocumented workers.

Milton Nain Guerra, 41, owner of Tool Master Home Improvement, is charged with running a corrupt organization following an investigation by Montoursville police.

It began after police took a man into custody on an unrelated issue and the man claimed his employer, Guerra, had confiscated his passport. The man also told police he and several other men lived and worked at a property owned by Guerra.

Police served a search warrants on July 28 at the property, located at 1244 State Route 87, on July 28. Eight people were taken into custody by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement during the searches. Although Guerra initially claimed the individuals were family members rather than employees, investigators said workers told them they lived at the property, drove company vehicles, and worked for the business. They were also dirty from "construction type work" and had paint on their clothing.

Employees told investigators they were paid in cash, with one reporting he earned $100 per day and another saying he earned $200 per day, or about $1,000 a week. They also reported working about six days a week without benefits and receiving the same hourly rate for overtime, according to the affidavit. They each paid Guerra $400 a month in rent, they alleged.

Investigators recovered handwritten payment ledgers dating to 2024 documenting between five and more than 10 employees and nearly $1 million in cash payments. Police said they found no tax records, W-2s, 1099s or payroll documents for the workers and recovered between $15,000 and $20,000 in cash from the two residences, including money set aside for employee wages. 

The affidavit also alleges workers were housed in crowded conditions, with up to three adult beds in a single bedroom. Investigators said Guerra distanced himself from the workers when questioned about their employment, pay, rent and benefits, and alleged the arrangement allowed him to underbid competitors while profiting from unlawful business practices.
