AES subsidiary nabs final siting permit for New York wind project

Kim Riley | August 3, 2026 | 2 min read
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by

Instaread

A final siting permit has been issued to Chateaugay NewCo LLC, a wholly owned subsidiary of AES Clean Energy, for a wind project in New York, according to the State Department of Public Service’s Office of Renewable Energy Siting and Electric Transmission (ORES).

The 116.5-megawatt (MW) Chateaugay Wind Project, located in the Town of Chateaugay in Franklin County and the Town of Clinton in Clinton County, will replace an existing facility that has reached the end of its useful life, the ORES said July 30. 

“The Chateaugay Wind Project demonstrates New York’s commitment to modernizing its clean energy infrastructure while making more efficient use of existing sites,” said Department of Public Service CEO Rory Christian. “Repowering projects like this allow us to increase renewable energy production, strengthen grid reliability, and expand access to some of the fastest to build, lowest-cost sources of new electricity while delivering meaningful economic benefits to the host communities.”

The existing wind turbines will be decommissioned and replaced with newer, more efficient, state-of-the-art turbines and associated infrastructure, with the new repowered facility expected to reduce the total number of turbines while delivering a generating capacity of 116.5 MW, approximately 10 MW more than the existing facility.

The repowered project will generate enough clean, renewable energy to power approximately 21,000 homes. The new turbines are expected to reach commercial operation in 2029, said ORES. 

“The repowering of the Chateaugay Wind Project will provide a net increase of clean, renewable energy into New York’s power grid while delivering lasting local economic benefits,” said ORES Interim Executive Director Jason Zehr. “I commend the community for its engagement on this project ahead of and throughout the ORES application process.” 

The Chateaugay Wind project will provide more than $15 million in direct financial benefits over the life of the project to the Town of Chateaugay and Franklin County. Under the Public Service Commission’s host community benefit program, the project is also expected to provide more than $1.16 million in utility bill credits for town residents over the first 10 years of operation.

Construction of the project will create approximately 77 full-time jobs, most of which are expected to be filled from the local labor market. Once operational, the facility will support a total of six permanent, full-time positions that will be hired locally.

The wind facility will consist of wind turbines and associated components, a medium-voltage electric collection system, gravel access roads, and meteorological towers, according to ORES.

With the approval of the Chateaugay Wind Project, ORES has now approved 37 large-scale solar and wind projects representing more than 5.4 GW of clean energy generation.

“ORES remains committed to ensuring that renewable energy projects are responsibly sited in a manner that protects public health, safety, and the environment while supporting a reliable and resilient electric grid,” Zehr said.
