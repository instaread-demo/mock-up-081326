Would you like to work at home as a transcriptionist? Are you confused about which transcription equipment beginners actually need?

Fortunately, you can start with fewer tools than you may think!

Many people are interested in working from home as a transcriptionist, but they are not always sure how much equipment or software to buy.

Video Muted

Transcription usually involves listening to recorded audio or video and turning it into an accurate written document. You may also need to research names and unfamiliar terms, follow a style guide, add timestamps, and identify different speakers.

AI has changed the industry, but human transcriptionists are still used for work that needs careful editing, good judgment, privacy, or better accuracy than an automated transcript can provide.

For most beginners, the essentials are a reliable computer, comfortable wired headphones, transcription playback software, and a word processor. You can usually wait to buy a foot pedal and text expander until you have paid work.

What Transcription Equipment Do Beginners Need?

You do not need to buy every possible tool before you apply for work. In fact, it is usually better to wait until you know what a company or client requires.

Most professional transcriptionists use some combination of the following:

  • A reliable computer that meets the company's requirements
  • Comfortable wired headphones or a transcription headset
  • Transcription playback software
  • A compatible foot pedal
  • Word-processing software, such as Microsoft Word
  • Text-expander software
  • Reliable high-speed internet
  • Secure storage and a backup system
  • Audio-cleanup software when the client allows it

Keep in mind that some companies provide their own online transcription system. If that is the case, you may not need separate playback software or a foot pedal.

You should also check the required operating system before accepting work or buying equipment. Express Scribe works on Windows and Mac, while the desktop version of FTW Transcriber is available for Windows. A Chromebook may not run the software a company requires.

How Do I Select a Headset?

I also have an article about headsets for remote work. However, transcription has different needs than phone-based customer service.

You are mainly listening while you transcribe, so you usually do not need a microphone. Clear speech, comfort, and a secure wired connection matter more.

Noise cancellation can mean different things. A noise-canceling microphone reduces what someone on a call hears, but that does not improve the audio you hear through the headphones.

If you work in a noisy room, closed-back or noise-isolating headphones may help block outside sound. Active noise cancellation could also help, but it is not required and may change how some audio sounds.

Wired headphones are usually the safest choice because they do not need charging and are less likely to disconnect. Before buying anything, check whether your computer has USB-A, USB-C, or a 3.5 mm headphone jack.

A few lower-cost options include:

  • Koss KSC75 — These are very lightweight, wired ear-clip headphones with clear sound. However, they have an open-air design and will not block much background noise.
  • Logitech H390 — This is a padded USB-A headset with inline volume controls. It includes a microphone, but the microphone's noise cancellation is mainly useful for calls rather than transcription.
  • Spectra USB Transcription Headset — This lightweight under-chin headset is made specifically for transcription. It has twin speakers, inline volume controls, a built-in sound card, and a long USB cord.

Prices change, and comfort is very personal. If possible, order from a seller with a reasonable return policy just so you can test the fit.

What Are Good Transcription Software Packages?

For traditional manual transcription, many professionals use Express Scribe by NCH Software.

Express Scribe offers playback-speed controls, hotkeys, support for many audio formats, and compatibility with professional foot pedals. It works in the background while you type in Microsoft Word or another word processor.

There is a free version, but note that some professional features and foot pedals require Express Scribe Pro. Check the software and pedal compatibility before buying either one.

FTW Transcriber is another option for Windows users. It supports hotkeys, timestamps, many file types, audio enhancement, and several popular foot pedals.

oTranscribe is a simpler browser-based option. Your audio and transcript stay in your browser rather than being uploaded to an oTranscribe server, but you should export your work often because browser storage is not meant for permanent files.

Whatever you choose, make sure your client allows it. Some clients require you to work only inside their own secure system.

Why Should You Invest in a Good Foot Pedal?

A foot pedal lets you start, stop, rewind, and fast-forward audio without taking your hands off the keyboard. It can definitely make long transcription files easier to manage.

That said, beginners can practice with keyboard hotkeys before buying one. A pedal becomes more useful when you start handling longer files or regular paid work.

The Infinity USB foot pedal is a popular professional option. Infinity pedals are supported by Express Scribe and FTW Transcriber, but the exact model, software license, and operating system matter.

For example, some Infinity pedals require the paid version of Express Scribe. Check the official compatibility list before ordering so that you do not end up with an expensive footrest.

What Is a Text Expander and Why Do You Need It?

A text expander replaces a short abbreviation with a longer word or phrase. For example, a shortcut could insert a repeated speaker label, company name, or formatting note.

This can save time when the same names and phrases appear throughout a file. But you still need to proofread everything because an incorrect expansion could create an error in the transcript.

TextExpander is a paid option that works on several platforms. PhraseExpress works on Windows, Mac, and iOS, while AutoHotkey is a free Windows automation tool that can also expand text.

Honestly, a text expander is helpful but not essential when you are first learning. Start with the tools your company requires and add time-saving software once you understand your workflow.

What About AI Transcription and Audio Cleanup?

Some transcriptionists now receive an AI-generated draft and are paid to correct it. Others still create the transcript manually from the original recording.

Do not upload a client's audio to an AI transcription site, cloud service, or other third-party tool unless the client has clearly given you permission. Recordings may contain private, medical, legal, or business information.

Audacity can reduce steady sounds such as hiss, humming, and fan noise. Use audio filters carefully, though, because aggressive cleanup can distort speech and make a difficult recording even harder to understand.

Also, do not assume you are allowed to change the source file. Always follow the client's security and file-handling rules.

Which Transcription Equipment Should You Buy First?

I hope this post has helped you better understand which transcription tools you may need!

You can begin practicing with a computer, a decent pair of headphones, playback software, and keyboard hotkeys. A professional headset, foot pedal, and text expander can be added later if you get regular work.

Also, check your company's requirements before purchasing anything. The wrong connection, operating system, pedal, or software license could leave you with equipment you cannot use.
